## 1. Foundational Patterns

### Tresillo
- **Grid (8th notes):** `x..x..x.`
- **Grouping:** 3+3+2
- **Context:** The most fundamental duple-pulse cell in Latin American music. African origin. Forms the "3-side" of son clave. Appears in reggaeton, New Orleans second line, modern pop.

### Habanera (Tango-Congo)
- **Grid (8th notes):** `x..x.xx.`
- **Grouping:** 3+3+1+1+2 (tresillo with beat 3 added)
- **Context:** Named after Havana. Cuban contradanza migrated to Argentina (tango), New Orleans (ragtime/jazz), Europe (Bizet's Carmen).

## 2. Clave Patterns

All claves are two-bar cycles in 4/4. Shown as 16-position grid (16th notes across 2 bars).

### Son Clave 3-2
- **Grid:** `x..x..x. ....x.x.`
- **Hits (1-indexed):** 1, 4, 7, 11, 13
- **Tempo:** Salsa 80-110, Son montuno 100-130
- **Context:** Foundation of Cuban son, salsa, mambo, Latin jazz. 3-side is tresillo, 2-side resolves.

### Son Clave 2-3
- **Grid:** `....x.x. x..x..x.`
- **Context:** Same pattern reversed. More laid-back feel, common in verse sections.

### Rumba Clave 3-2
- **Grid:** `x..x..x. .....xx.`
- **Hits:** 1, 4, 7, 12, 13
- **Context:** Used in rumba, guaguanco, mozambique, songo, timba. More folkloric than son clave.

### Rumba Clave 2-3
- **Grid:** `.....xx. x..x..x.`

### Bossa Nova Clave
- **Grid:** `x..x..x. ..x..x..`
- **Hits:** 1, 4, 7, 10, 13
- **Tempo:** 110-140 BPM
- **Context:** Backbone of bossa nova. More even/flowing than son clave.

### Bembe Bell (6/8)
- **Grid (12-step):** `x.xx.xx.xx.x`
- **Tempo:** 100-140 BPM (dotted-quarter pulse)
- **Context:** 7-stroke bell pattern. Most common timeline in sub-Saharan Africa. Rolling, circular feel.

## 3. Genre Grooves — Instrument Layers

### Salsa (80-110 BPM, 4/4)
- **Clave:** Son clave (2-3 or 3-2), often implied
- **Tumbao (Congas):** Open tones on "and of 4", slaps on beat 2. `h.S.h.OO h.S.h.OO`
- **Cascara (Timbales shell):** `x.xx.xx. xx.xx.x.` — elaboration of clave
- **Campana (Bongo bell):** Accents on strong beats + syncopated fills, during loud sections
- **Montuno (Piano):** Syncopated repeating chord pattern (guajeo) locked to clave
- **Bass Tumbao:** Root an 8th note before downbeat — perpetual "ahead of the beat"
- **Guiro:** Steady scraping accenting strong beats

### Bossa Nova (110-145 BPM, 4/4)
- **Cross-stick:** Bossa nova clave pattern
- **Hi-hat:** Steady 8th notes (replicating shaker/ganza)
- **Bass drum:** Beats 1 and 3 (variations with "and of 2")
- **Guitar:** Bass notes beats 1+3, syncopated chord stabs around them
- **Feel:** Intimate, understated. Samba DNA slowed and internalized.

### Samba (96-152 BPM, 2/4)
- **Surdo (low):** Beat 2 (offbeat emphasis — African root)
- **Surdo (high):** Beat 1
- **Surdo (mid/cutador):** Improvises between
- **Caixa (snare):** Continuous 16th-note roll with ghost notes/accents
- **Tamborim:** `x..x..x.x...x..x` (partido alto-derived)
- **Agogo bells:** Two-pitched alternating pattern
- **Chocalho/Ganza (shaker):** Continuous 16ths
- **Repinique:** Lead drum, calls and breaks
- **Suingue:** Unique swing between straight 16ths and triplets — not notatable, must be felt

### Cha-Cha-Cha (108-132 BPM, 4/4)
- **Signature:** Triple on beats 4-and-1 ("cha-cha-CHA")
- **Guiro:** Long scrape beats 1+3, two short scrapes on "ands" `1 2& 3 4&`
- **Cowbell:** Steady quarter notes on timbale bell
- **Clave:** Son clave (usually 2-3)

### Mambo (104-200 BPM, 4/4)
- **Accents:** Beats 2 and 4
- **Timbales:** Cascara and bell patterns prominent
- **Horns:** Syncopated riffs (mambos/monas)
- **Congas:** Tumbao
- **Clave:** Son clave (2-3 or 3-2)

### Cumbia (80-150 BPM, 2/4 or 4/4)
- **Kick:** Beat 1
- **Snare/rimshot:** Beat 2
- **Guacharaca (scraper):** Steady 16ths with accents
- **Bass:** Simple root-fifth
- **Feel:** Lilting, "galloping" 2-feel
- **Variants:** Colombian traditional (~80-100), Mexican (~90-120), Villera (~100-130)

### Merengue (120-180 BPM, 2/4)
- **Tambora:** Alternating stick hits and palm slaps, offbeat emphasis. "Quintillo" figure (5 rapid hits) at transitions
- **Guira:** Steady 16th-note scraping with accent patterns
- **Bass:** Simple root on downbeats
- **Feel:** Relentless, driving, every beat strongly articulated

### Reggaeton / Dembow (85-100 BPM, 4/4)
- **Kick:** `x...x...x...x...` (four-on-the-floor)
- **Snare:** `...x..x.....x..x` (positions 4, 7, 12, 15 — tresillo cross-rhythm)
- **Origin:** Panama late 1980s, Puerto Rico 1990s-2000s. Named after Shabba Ranks' "Dem Bow" (1991).

### Guaguanco (100-120 BPM, 4/4)
- **Clave:** Rumba clave (usually 3-2)
- **Palitos/cascara:** Timeline on wood surface
- **Tumbadora (low conga):** Steady ostinato
- **Tres-dos (middle conga):** Counter-clave
- **Quinto (high conga):** Improvises — the lead voice

### Mozambique (100-125 BPM, 4/4)
- **Clave:** 2-3 rumba clave
- **Bell:** Derived from guaguanco cascara
- **Instruments:** Congas, bombos (bass drums), cowbells, trombones
- **Context:** Created by Pello el Afrokan (1963). Two styles: Cuban street carnival, NY adaptation (Eddie Palmieri).

### Songo (~100 BPM half-note, 4/4)
- **Clave:** Loosely 2-3 rumba
- **Cymbal bell:** Half-note pulse
- **Bass drum:** Tumbao rhythm (syncopated)
- **Snare:** Syncopated fills creating linear pattern
- **Context:** 1970s, Los Van Van / Irakere. Brought folkloric rumba into dance bands.

## 4. Instrument Layer Roles

| Layer | Role | Salsa | Samba | Bossa Nova |
|-------|------|-------|-------|------------|
| Timeline/Clave | Rhythmic DNA | Clave sticks | Tamborim | Cross-stick |
| Bell/Scraper | Timekeeper | Campana / guiro | Agogo | Shaker |
| Low drum | Foundation | Conga open tones | Surdo | Bass drum |
| Mid percussion | Texture | Timbales cascara | Caixa | Hi-hat 8ths |
| High drum | Energy/improv | Bongo martillo | Repinique | — |
| Bass | Harmonic anchor | Tumbao (anticipated) | Simple root | Walking/pedal |
| Harmonic rhythm | Groove engine | Piano montuno | Cavaquinho strum | Guitar pattern |
